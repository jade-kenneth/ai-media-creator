# User journeys

## J1. Mika's first video brief (activation)

**Who:** Mika Reyes, a TikTok Shop affiliate in Quezon City, making Taglish lifestyle videos. She has a BlendGo Mini listing link and three phone photos.

| Moment | Mika does | She sees | She feels | Design response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Arrive | Opens the app from a link | A sign-in panel with one Google button | “One click, fine.” | Google only; first sign-in sets up the workspace |
| Empty dashboard | Looks for where to start | “Make your first affiliate video” with three steps | Oriented | One primary action |
| Product | Pastes the listing link | 4 of 7 fields filled, missing ones named | Relieved, but checks | Imported badges; never overwrites; manual always works |
| Media | Ticks the rights box, drops photos | Per-file progress; one file too big | Mildly annoyed | Exact reason and limit on the tile |
| Facts | Reviews 8 facts | “Blends ice in 10 seconds” is flagged | Cautious | Flag explains why; she decides |
| Strategy | Types her buyer; asks for angles | Three angles, each showing the facts it uses | In control | Cost shown before the click |
| Script | Waits, then edits | Three hooks; scene 4 flagged; 34 s of 30 s | Busy but guided | Claim check jumps to the line; length meter |
| Approve | Approves v3 | Brief ready | Done | Approval locks the version |
| Brief | Copies the brief to film | Plain text with a shot list | Ready to shoot | Copy and download |

**Success measure:** percentage of new creators who finish fact review and approve a script (activation).

## J2. Returning after a failed job

**Who:** Mika, on the LED Desk Lamp project. Script writing failed overnight because the service timed out.

1. The dashboard card shows “Script writing failed. Open to retry.” in red.
2. She opens it; the job panel explains what happened, that her facts and strategy are safe and that she wasn't charged.
3. **Try again · 3 credits** reuses the same job; the panel moves to Queued then Running.
4. She leaves for another project; when she returns the script is ready.

**Design response:** failure copy states what happened, what was kept and what to do next; retries never create a second job.

## J3. Testing another hook

**Who:** Mika, happy with the blender video, wants a version that opens with the “bag” visual.

1. Duplicates the project from the dashboard.
2. The copy has the same product, facts and strategy; the approved script arrives as a new draft.
3. Picks the second hook, edits scene 1, approves.
4. Both projects now have their own approved brief.

**Success measure:** percentage of creators who return to make a new hook variant (retention).

## J4. An approved fact changes

**Who:** Mika learns the blender weighs 450 g, not 420 g.

1. Edits the fact on Fact review. A banner warns that the approved script uses it.
2. The fact goes back to *Needs review*; the approved version becomes *Needs review* too.
3. Approves the fact; on Script Studio the banner points to the scene that used it; she re-approves.
4. The brief shows the updated version.

**Design response:** upstream edits never silently change approved work, and never leave stale approvals looking valid.

## J5. Mika's first story (Entertainment Studio, §3.23)

**Who:** Mika, who also posts short comedy bits. She wants a 45-second Taglish skit with a friend, with no product in it.

| Moment | Mika does | She sees | She feels | Design response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| New video | Presses **New video** | “What are you making?” with Affiliate video and Story | Knows where to go | One card per built studio; nothing “coming soon” |
| Genre | Picks Comedy | “A setup, a turn and a punchline. Timing does the work.” | Focused | One genre per project; the hint sets expectations |
| Premise | Gives AI one small umbrella detail and asks for premises, then chooses one or writes her own | Three complete ideas and casts built around her detail | Guided without having to write the whole story | Suggestions cost 1 credit; the optional detail persists and makes old suggestions stale when changed; her own full premise always works (R30) |
| Cast | Adds Ana and Ben with their looks | “2 of 4”, the story rules beside the form | Clear on the limits | Fictional characters only; never a public figure |
| Script | Writes, picks a hook, approves v1 | Setup, Build-up, Turn, then a Cliffhanger: Ben finds out Ana is 4B and asks who has been leaving notes on his door; lines acted as beats; no claim check | Hooked on her own story | A story has no facts, so approval needs only a hook; every story ends on a cliffhanger (R29) |
| Brief | Films with her friend | The cast one per line and “Get permission from everyone who appears.” | Ready to shoot | The brief is the shooting script |
| Video | Adds clips, turns on an end line | “The Umbrella Standoff · Part 2 tomorrow” | Proud | The end card is off until she wants it; no #ad |
| Episode 2 (§3.25) | Presses **Next episode**, picks one of three ideas for what happens next, writes and approves | “Ana admits the notes were hers, and Ben has one of his own.”; the first scene picks up Ben's question | Hooked on continuing | The cast and looks carry over; each episode ends on a new cliffhanger until she marks one Final |

**Design response:** the shared steps don't assume a product, and each studio's own copy lives in its steps. **Success measure:** percentage of creators who finish a story from New video to an approved script.
