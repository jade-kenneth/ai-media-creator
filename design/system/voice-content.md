# Voice and content

## Personality

A confident, practical creative tool for working creators. It is fast and trustworthy, not hype-driven. We’re honest about AI: **it drafts, the creator decides.**

## Principles

1. **Plain and direct.** Short sentences. Say what happens: “Rewrites only this scene.”
2. **Affirming, never salesy.** No “magic”, “effortless”, “viral” or “guaranteed”. We never promise sales or compliance.
3. **The creator is in control.** “Approve only what you can stand behind.” “Nothing is final until you approve a version.”
4. **Errors say three things:** what happened, what was kept, and what to do next.
   > “Script writing didn’t finish. The writing service timed out after 60 seconds. Your facts, strategy and earlier versions are safe.” [Try again]
5. **Costs are never hidden.** Paid actions show their estimate on the button. Completions state what was used.

## Mechanics

- UI copy is **English**. Script, caption and voice **content** may be English, Filipino or Taglish, and language selectors are labelled “Script language”.
- Sentence case for everything, including buttons, titles and menu items.
- Buttons are verb-first and specific: “Write hooks & script”, “Continue to facts”, “Restore as v4”, “Remove file”. Avoid “OK”, “Submit” and “Yes”.
- **Destructive confirmations** name the object (“Remove blendgo-front.jpg?”), state the consequence and whether it can be undone, and label the buttons “Keep file” / “Remove file”.
- **Disabled primary actions** always show the reason next to them (“2 facts still need review”).
- Use curly quotes (’ “ ”), an en dash for time ranges (0:08–0:14) and a middle dot for meta lists (Edited 12 min ago · No exports yet). Avoid em dashes in UI copy.
- Numbers: “380 ml”, “30 s”, “₱899”, and “3 credits” (always with the unit).
- Relative time for recent events (“12 min ago”, “yesterday”). Absolute dates beyond a week (“20 Sep”).

## Claim-safety copy

- **Flags** open with a category (“Performance claim.”, “New claim: …”), then the reason. They never accuse: “It isn’t one of your approved facts.” (Affiliate Studio only: a story has no facts and no flags, §3.23 R28 D7.)
- A listing is “source material, not proof”. We never say a fact is “verified”.
- **Reminders** close with “These are reminders, not a compliance check. The final review is yours.”
- **Disclosure (Affiliate Studio):** “Add the affiliate disclosure your platform requires.” We never claim the platform handles it. A story has no affiliate disclosure and no `#ad` (§3.23).
- **Story reminders (Entertainment Studio, §3.23):** the brief's `BEFORE YOU FILM AND POST` block and its aside card “Before you film and post”: “Get permission from everyone who appears.” · “Fake fights and stunts with angles and cuts. Never film real danger.” · “Label AI-generated scenes if your platform asks for it.” The Export card “Before you post on TikTok”: “Add TikTok's AI-generated label when a scene uses an AI clip or voice.” · “Make sure everyone who appears agreed to be in the video.” · “Don't present the story as something that really happened.” Both close with the reminders line above.
- **Story rules (R28 D6):** stated as plain reminders, never as accusations: “Stories are fiction. Don't present one as real news.” · “Action and scares stay stylised, with no gore.” · “No sexual content, and no one under 18 in romance or violence.” · “Never use a real public figure as a character.” A character photo's confirmation reads “This is me, someone who agreed to appear in AI video, or a character I have the rights to.” with “Never a real public figure or anyone under 18.”
- **Studio-neutral surfaces (§3.23):** sign-in, the dashboard and the app shell never name a fixed set of studios and never promise only product videos. Studio copy lives in that studio's steps.
